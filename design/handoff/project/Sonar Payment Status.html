External payment status — four directions

Paying a scanned invoice or an off-app destination hides a lot of machinery: resolving the address, finding a Lightning route, an HTLC in flight, then a settlement proof. Today the app just says “sending”, so the user can’t tell what stage they’re at or whether their money is at risk.

The question every state must answer: what is happening right now, where is my money (still mine / in flight / gone / refunded), and what do I do next. “Failed” is never enough on its own.
State

A · Stepped ledger

Trust through transparency
Shows the real stages as a receipt that fills in. The pinned money line is the emotional anchor and always names where the sats are.
  • Best for: first-time external payments, larger amounts
  • Risk: can feel technical if the copy slips

B · One sentence

Calm minimal, details on demand
One ring, one plain-language headline, one money pill. The technical trace hides behind “What’s happening?” so power users can still audit.
  • Best for: everyday small payments
  • Risk: hides progress detail when things stall

C · Journey rail

Spatial metaphor, glanceable
The coin physically travels you → recipient. On failure it visibly comes back, which answers “where’s my money” without reading a word.
  • Best for: comprehension at a glance, demos
  • Risk: motion must never imply success early

D · Resumable status

You can leave — it keeps working
Dismissible sheet plus a live wallet row that survives navigation, with explicit next actions per terminal state (proof, retry, refund).
  • Best for: slow/stuck Lightning payments
  • Risk: two surfaces to keep in sync

Where does it live on home?

An external payment has no chat thread — there's no conversation with a scanned QR code. So it needs its own place in the list. Same state switcher drives all three; each opens Direction B.

H1 · Pinned strip

Above the list, only while active
A tinted card pinned above Messages. Present only while in flight or needing attention; clears itself a few seconds after success.
  • Wins: impossible to miss, doesn't pollute history
  • Cost: pushes conversations down

H2 · A row in the list

Payments are conversations too
The payment sits inline, sorted by time, with a gold coin tile. It stays as history after settling — tap for the receipt.
  • Wins: one mental model, permanent record
  • Cost: less urgent; can scroll away

H3 · Slim bar

Like an active call
A full-width bar under the title, borrowing the call-in-progress idiom. Highest urgency, smallest footprint.
  • Wins: zero layout disruption, reads as system state
  • Cost: too loud for a routine 2,100 sats
